## Why this decision and not the famous one

Every question she has been asked since June 2022 is about food or about being the first. The
first Native Hawaiian woman to win a James Beard award, the first Hawaii-born winner in nineteen
years, what she does with ulu, how much of the menu is local, where she eats on her day off. Her
own answer to the first-ever question is always to deflect credit to her team, which is a sign it
has been asked past the point of interest.

The fifteen New York years are treated as backstory to skip past, and they contain the actual
decision. She moved to New York for a master's, stayed fifteen years, and spent most of them
teaching rather than cooking: roughly five years running the culinary program at the Art Institute
of New York City, then co-founding the culinary program at CUNY Kingsborough with Jonathan Deutsch.
Middlebury's own profile puts it plainly, that she "was secure in a tenure track position as part
of that beloved university system." She left it in 2015 and opened Fête in Chinatown in March 2016,
in her forties, in a 1,700 square foot corner space at 2 N. Hotel St.

The verifier found no interview that asks about Kingsborough, about teaching cooking to community
college students, or about giving up tenure. That is the open space.

The question is a real counterfactual rather than a disguised compliment. It does not assume the
restaurant was the right call, and she is the only person who can answer it.

## Max facts used, and why

- **Middlebury** leads the subject line and the opening clause. The connection is confirmed and
  unusually live: class of 1996, joint major in modern dance and English, and she is a **2026**
  Alumni Achievement Award winner who was physically on campus for Reunion in June 2026, ten weeks
  before this email, co-curating a tasting event with Rob Tod '91.
- **Building an AI teaching assistant** is the real overlap, not decoration. She built two culinary
  programs from nothing and holds an NYU master's in food studies. The read and the hook share the
  word: her restaurant is cast as a classroom, and the question is about what she would be
  teaching. The overlap is visible in the email, not just in the research.
- The email deliberately does **not** list the $5K, the $30k cold calling, or the turned-down money.
  Three credentials to a chef who has heard every version of a young person's pitch reads as a
  résumé.

Deliberately unused:

- **The James Beard award.** It is the single most-cited fact about her and it is in every email she
  gets. Naming it would have made this one indistinguishable.
- **Buenos Aires, Barcelona, tennis.** The verifier searched and found nothing. Inventing proximity
  is worse than leaving it out.
- **Heyday**, the 2021 White Sands Hotel restaurant. The hotel's dining page no longer names her or
  Fête, and no dated source confirms or refutes her involvement. Unresolved facts do not go in.
- **A published 2013 cookbook.** Asserted by Wikipedia only, no publisher or library catalogue page
  found. Untraceable, so excluded.
- **The seven years she and her husband spent trying to have children**, which is published and is
  connected to the restaurant's origin. Not material for a cold email from a stranger.
- **"Opened with no press."** Middlebury's page and Honolulu Magazine say she opened without a
  name-brand chef or a media blitz, but Honolulu Magazine itself ran a First Look a week after
  opening, so "no press" would have been a claim she could disprove from memory. Cut.
