## Why this decision and not the famous one

Her public life has two stories in it and both are burned to the ground. The secret school in
Kabul, where she dressed as a boy to get to class, opens every profile she has ever had: TEDWomen
2012, TEDWomen 2021, 60 Minutes, NPR, National Geographic twice. Burning her students' records in
August 2021 and evacuating 250 people to Rwanda is the second, and it is the one every outlet since
has led with. An email built on either would be indistinguishable from the rest of her inbox.

The decision used instead is one almost nobody has written about. SOLA did not start as a school.
From 2008 it was a scholarship pipeline sending high-performing Afghan students to universities
abroad, and it worked: it helped secure the equivalent of more than $10 million in scholarships.
Then, per the New York Times obituary of her co-founder Ted Achilles:

> "By 2012, concerned the program was encouraging brain drain, they decided to transform SOLA into
> a boarding high school for girls from across the country, especially those from rural areas."

She killed a funded, functioning program because its success was the problem, and it then took four
more years to open the school, in March 2016, with two dozen sixth graders. She argued the position
publicly at Harvard in February 2014. As far as the research could find, this sequence appears in
exactly one place, and interviewers routinely jump from "founded in 2008" to "opened in 2016"
without asking what filled the eight years between.

The question is a real counterfactual and one she can actually answer, because she personally
placed those students. It asks her to look at the evidence for the road she did not take.
