### Career trajectory, in order

1. Middlebury 2001-2005, BA economics, football.
2. **Knight Capital, Jersey City**, first job out of college, trading health care stocks. The
   unglamorous one, and a market-making shop rather than a Manhattan brand name. No dates sourced.
3. CFA charter, then **Point72 / S.A.C. Capital** as a day trader. He calls it "the seat I'd wanted
   since I was 20 years old."
4. **~September 2013**, quits drinking while training for a first ultramarathon.
5. **2015-2017**, researches brewing and writes business plans on nights and weekends **while still
   employed at the hedge fund**.
6. **~2017**, meets co-founder John Walker, a Santa Fe head brewer, through a deliberately vague ad
   on an online brewing forum.
7. **2017**, leaves the hedge fund, puts in his life savings, raises $3M from friends and angels.
   He and Walker test-brew **100+ batches on homebrew equipment** and hand-bottle in Walker's
   parents' garage.
8. **May 2018**, commercial launch with two beers. Six employees at year end.
9. **2020**, buys the former Ballast Point brewery in San Diego, hires 100 people during the
   pandemic.
10. **2022**, opens a 150,000 sq ft Milford CT brewery. Keurig Dr Pepper takes a $50M minority stake
    at just under a $500M valuation.
11. **July 2024**, $50M led by General Atlantic at **~$800M**, roughly double the prior mark.
12. Now ~300 employees, bicoastal breweries, 75,000+ retail doors, Certified B Corp, all staff hold
    equity.

**The interesting shape:** Knight Capital to Point72 is the only conventionally upward move in the
whole sequence. Everything after it is lateral or downward on paper. And he says the finance
toolkit did not transfer at all: "I thought I was this like super smart finance guy coming into the
beer world and ultimately none of those skills ended up being transferable."

### The decision used in the email

He quit drinking in 2013 and then built the category leader by **refusing to market to people who
quit drinking**, aiming at drinkers instead. He raised this himself on 2026-06-17, unprompted, and
named the opposite approach as one of the two biggest risks to the whole category:

> "the category 10 years ago was a designated drinker, sober messaged category that was in the
> penalty box. And I think we did a lot of work to push it out of that and like bring it to a
> positive aspirational thing. I think one of the more dangerous things about all these celebrity
> brands is all of them are talking about sobriety, and almost reinforcing a sobriety message for
> the category, which is actually a really small part of the consumers."

Three other documented hard calls were available and not used: deliberately holding back from gas
and convenience retail, which is 50%+ of beer volume; spending $150M+ on owned breweries when the
CPG playbook says go asset-light and contract brewers had rejected them; and leaving Point72.
