**Middlebury: confirmed, class of 1991, geology degree.** Named one of Middlebury's **2026

and therefore strong evidence of class year and affiliation.
Source: `middlebury.edu/alumni-and-families/rob-tod-91-and-robynne-maii-96-named-2026-alumni-achievement-award-winners`

**Alive and in role.** The 2026 award announcement describes him in the present tense as an
involved leader at Allagash. Allagash's own site describes him as founder. A separate 2025 item
records Allagash brewing with more than two million pounds of Maine-grown grain in 2025, against

**The path, from two independent sources.**
- Middlebury 1991, geology degree, moved to Colorado for two years, came back to Vermont and took
  a job **washing kegs at Otter Creek Brewing in Middlebury**, where co-workers introduced him to
  Belgian beer. (Boston Globe, 2025-02-25, "How Allagash White conquered the world"; corroborated
  by Allagash's own "Story Behind Allagash White".)
- April 1995: rented a corner of a warehouse in an industrial park, **welded together a 15-barrel
  brewing system himself and jackhammered drains into the floor**, and brewed one beer only, a
  Belgian-style wheat ale. He was brewer, keg cleaner and salesman.
  (`allagash.com/about/our-story/`)

**The decision, in his own words.** Allagash White failed at first. Tod, in a Forbes interview

> "The first reaction I usually got when I poured it was what's wrong with it? Why does it look

him: "Rob, why don't you do something more accessible? Why don't you make something that can
sell?" He refused:
> "I just didn't feel like giving into that, I believed in what we were doing, and it finally got
> traction."

He won the **James Beard Award for Outstanding Wine, Beer or Spirits Producer in 2019.**

**The read.** Leaving a geology degree to wash kegs, then betting a whole brewery on one hazy
beer bartenders kept saying would never sell, is conviction that took years to look correct.

**The question.** How he told the difference between the market being wrong and him being wrong.
Deliberately *not* "why didn't you pivot", which he has answered in Forbes and elsewhere. The
version asked here is about the epistemics of the call, not the outcome, and Max has the same
problem in front of him right now.
