## Email grading

Published addresses were searched for first, in the order the skill sets out. Company team pages,
personal sites and public artifacts were all checked before Hunter was touched:

- `lcatterton.com`, `adventinternational.com`, `hebbia.com`, `hebbia.ai` team and contact pages:
  no individual addresses published.
- `rauchg.com` and `rauchg.com/about`: fetched and scanned, no address.
- **GitHub API on `vercel/next.js`:** Rauch's own commits carry `rauchg@gmail.com` as the author
  address, across five commits. This is a public artifact he authored, which the skill grades
  MEDIUM.

Then Hunter, for the four with nothing published:

| Address | Hunter score | SMTP | accept_all | Control probe | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jon.owsley@lcatterton.com | 95 | valid, 100 | false | **could not be run**, see below | MEDIUM |
| ali@adventinternational.com | 99 | valid, 92 | false | nonsense mailbox rejected | MEDIUM |
| rauchg@gmail.com | n/a, from commits | valid, 90 | false | n/a, published address | MEDIUM |
| guillermo.rauch@vercel.com | 82 | valid | **true** | meaningless | discarded |
| sridhar.ramaswamy@snowflake.com | 80 | valid | **true** | meaningless | GUESSED, blank |
| George Sivulka @ hebbia.ai | no result | n/a | n/a | n/a | GUESSED, blank |

**The control test, and where it failed.** The method from the second run is to probe a nonsense
mailbox at the same domain first; if the junk address is rejected and `accept_all` is false, a
"valid" result means the mailbox exists. That worked on `adventinternational.com`:
`zqx9plarb@adventinternational.com` came back undeliverable, score 0, while `ali@` came back
deliverable at 92.

On `lcatterton.com` the probe **could not be completed**. Two different nonsense mailboxes both
returned Hunter error 222, "we failed to verify for reasons outside of our control", while
`jon.owsley@` verified cleanly at score 100 with `smtp_check: true` and `accept_all: false`. The
asymmetry is suggestive but it is not the control test, so this address is graded MEDIUM on
Hunter's own catch-all determination alone, one notch weaker in evidence than Li's despite the
higher score. Flagging it rather than burying it.

**`guillermo.rauch@vercel.com` was found and then discarded** in favour of the commit address,
because vercel.com is a catch-all and the commit address is a real published artifact. The
trade-off is the one the skill names: a commit address is often a tagged alias the owner filters.

Deliverable: **3 of 5**. Consistent with the brief's expectation for this altitude.
