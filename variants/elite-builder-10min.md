## What this is

The only variant that leads with **what Max has actually shipped** rather than what he wants to
learn. The subject line changes too: it describes the work, not the asker.

## Hypothesis

Against `cold-midd-personal-10min`: at the top of the market, "hungry to learn" is the most common
sentence in the inbox and carries no information. **Evidence of work does.** A founder who built
something from nothing responds to proof that the sender is also building, because it converts the
email from a request into a peer signal, however asymmetric the altitude.

The trade: it spends words on Max that the standard variant spends on them. That is the whole
experiment. If it loses, the lesson is that at this level nobody cares what a 20-year-old has
built, and the read is the only currency.

## Reference shape

```
Hi {first_name},

I'm Max, a Middlebury sophomore. I built an AI teaching assistant for our STEM courses, won $5K at
MiddChallenge for it, and I'm partway through Entrepreneur First interviews.

{the read - one sentence, a judgment about what they built}

{the question - something a builder would ask another builder}

If you're open to 10 minutes, I promise I'll pay it forward.

Best,
Max
```

## Budget

**90-100 words.** The top of the range, because the proof needs specifics and specifics cost
words. It does not go beyond 100.

## Rules

- **Facts with numbers, never adjectives.** "$5K at MiddChallenge" works. "A successful AI
  project" does not. If a claim has no number, date or name in it, cut it.
- **Three items maximum.** A list of five reads as a résumé, and a résumé reads as someone asking
  for a job.
- **Everything must be true today.** The credentials live in `BRIEF.md` under current context;
  read them from there rather than from memory, because they go stale. Do not claim a win that has
  not happened or an interview stage not reached.
- **The question has to earn the setup.** Having just spent 30 words on himself, the question must
  be one only a builder would think to ask. A generic "what advice would you give" wastes the
  whole variant.
- House rules unchanged: no em dashes, no availability windows, no banned phrases.

## When not to use it

For people whose eminence is institutional rather than entrepreneurial, a bank chairman or a
museum director, leading with a student project reads as naive. Use `cold-midd-personal-10min`
there.
