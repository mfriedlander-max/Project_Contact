## What this is

The shortest thing that can still work. Roughly **55 words**, against 85 for the standard cold
email.

Built for people who get hundreds of these. At that altitude the scarce resource is not their
interest, it is the three seconds they spend deciding whether to keep reading.

## Hypothesis

Against `cold-midd-personal-10min`: for very senior people, **brevity reads as respect and as
confidence**. A long email says the sender needed room to justify himself. A four-line email says
he knows exactly what he wants and values your time more than his own explanation.

The risk it tests: cutting to 55 words means dropping either the read or the credential. This
variant drops **the credential**, keeping only the read and the question, on the theory that a
stranger's résumé is the least interesting thing in the message.

If this loses to the standard variant, the credential was load-bearing after all, which is worth
knowing.

## Reference shape

```
Hi {first_name},

{Max here, 20, + one identifying clause. Use "Middlebury sophomore" only with a confirmed
connection, otherwise "a sophomore studying applied math" or a hook from BRIEF.md} building an
AI teaching assistant.

{the read - one sentence, a judgment about a specific decision they made}

{the question - one sentence}

10 minutes? I'll pay it forward.

Best,
Max
```

## Budget

| Part | Words |
|---|---|
| Who Max is | ~11 |
| The read | 18-22 |
| The question | 15-20 |
| The close | 6 |
| **Total** | **~55, hard cap 65** |

## Rules

- **One sentence per idea.** No sentence carries two clauses joined by "and then".
- **No throat-clearing.** No "I hope this finds you well", no "I know you're busy". Both cost
  words and say nothing.
- The close is deliberately clipped. "10 minutes? I'll pay it forward." is the whole ask.
- Everything in the house rules still applies: no em dashes, no availability windows, no banned
  phrases, judgment rather than compliment.

## When not to use it

When the read genuinely needs two sentences to land, use `cold-midd-personal-10min` instead.
Cramming a complex judgment into 20 words produces something that sounds clever and says nothing,
which is worse than being 30 words longer.
