## What this is

The current house style, taken from an email Max actually wrote. It replaces the older
`cold-*-10min` variants, which stated a fact about the recipient and stopped there.

## What changed, and why it matters

The old variants said what someone did. This one says **what Max makes of it**. Compare:

> old: "You founded Pinboard."
> new: "Spending three decades at Goldman and then pivoting to lead research on the economics
>       of AI infrastructure is strategic and smart."

The second proves he actually looked at the career and formed a view. The first proves he read
a headline. That judgment is the whole email - everything else is scaffolding.

The ask also changed. It is now a **real question Max wants answered**, not a request for time
in the abstract. "What most people get wrong about where the real value in AI will land" is
something the recipient has an opinion about and probably enjoys giving.

## Reference email

```
Hi George,

I'm Max, 20, ambitious, curious about entrepreneurship, and a current Midd kid. Spending three decades at Goldman and then pivoting to lead research on the economics of AI infrastructure is strategic and smart.

I've been building out an AI teaching assistant for Middlebury, so I'm hungry to learn and would love to understand what you think most people get wrong about where the real value in AI will land. If you're open to 10 minutes, I promise I'll pay it forward.

Best,
Max
```

## Structure

1. `Hi {first_name},`
2. **Who Max is** - age, posture, Middlebury. Kept to one clause; it is not the point.
3. **The read on them** - their path plus a judgment about it. One or two sentences.
4. **What Max is building** that makes the question genuine.
5. **The question** - specific, opinionated, answerable.
6. `If you're open to 10 minutes, I promise I'll pay it forward.`
7. `Best,` / `Max`

## Rules

- **The judgment must be defensible.** "Strategic and smart" works because pivoting from
  finance to AI research is a real, describable move. Do not praise; assess.
- **The question must be one only they can answer well.** If it could be asked of any of the
  other nine people that day, it is too generic.
- **Never** "I came across", "I noticed", "your remarkable/impressive", "I would be honored",
  "resonates with me".
- **No em dashes.** Standing rule, per `email_personalization_prompt.md`. Use "and", a comma,
  or split it into two sentences.
- **No availability windows.** The older template listed three time slots. These do not - it
  reads as presumptuous before someone has agreed to talk.
- Keep it under 120 words.
