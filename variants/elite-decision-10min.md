## What this is

Instead of summarising a career, this picks **one specific decision** the person made and asks
about the road not taken.

Not "you built X and it is impressive" but "you chose X over Y at a moment when Y was the obvious
choice, and I want to know what you saw."

## Hypothesis

Against `cold-midd-personal-10min`: a career summary, however well judged, is still something the
recipient already knows and has read about themselves many times. **A counterfactual is not.**
Nobody has asked them what would have happened if they had done the other thing, and most people
find that question genuinely interesting to answer.

It also proves research more efficiently. Knowing someone founded a company proves you read a
headline. Knowing they turned down an acquisition in 2019, or moved a company out of San
Francisco, or shipped a feature that cannibalised their own product, proves you actually looked.

## Reference shape

```
Hi {first_name},

{who Max is - one clause. Middlebury form ONLY if they have a confirmed connection, otherwise a
hook from BRIEF.md} {the specific decision, stated plainly, with what made it hard}

I'm building an AI teaching assistant, so I'm hungry to learn and would love to
know {what would have happened / what you saw that others did not / what you would decide
differently now}. If you're open to 10 minutes, I promise I'll pay it forward.

Best,
Max
```

## Budget

Same band as the standard variant: **90-95 words.** The decision gets one sentence, not three.

## Finding the decision

Look for a moment where the obvious choice and the taken choice diverged:

- turning down money, an acquisition, or a title
- leaving somewhere successful before they had to
- building the thing that undercut their own business
- staying somewhere unfashionable when leaving was the norm
- publishing something inconvenient for their own position

**It must be documented.** A decision you infer is a decision you invented. If research cannot
point to where the choice is described, this variant does not apply and the standard one does.

## Rules

- **State the decision, do not praise it.** "You moved the company out of San Francisco in 2021"
  is the raw material; the judgment is what you make of it.
- **The question must be a real counterfactual**, not a disguised compliment. "How did you know it
  would work?" assumes it worked and flatters. "What would have happened if you had stayed?" does
  not.
- Never speculate about a decision's motive in the email. Ask about it instead. That is the point
  of the variant.
- House rules unchanged: no em dashes, no availability windows, no banned phrases.
